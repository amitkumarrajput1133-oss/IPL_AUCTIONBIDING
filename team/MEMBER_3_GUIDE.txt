4. YOUR CODE FILES & WHAT THEY DO (IN PLAIN ENGLISH)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- model/ (User, Team, Player, Bid, Auction): 
  The database blueprints. Defines table columns, foreign keys (@ManyToOne, 
  @OneToMany), and uses BigDecimal for financial figures.

- repository/PlayerRepository.java: 
  Contains @Lock(LockModeType.PESSIMISTIC_WRITE). Tells the database: 
  "Lock this player row so nobody else can change their price until this bid commits."

- service/BidService.java: 
  The bidding engine brain. Checks 4 rules: 
  1) Does team have enough budget? 
  2) Is new bid higher than current bid? 
  3) Is this team trying to outbid itself? 
  4) Is it above the base price?

- controller/BidController.java: 
  Provides URLs (POST /api/v1/bids/place, GET /api/v1/bids/live/{playerId}) 
  to place and fetch bids.

- config/DataInitializer.java: 
  Auto-seeds the database on startup with all 10 official IPL franchises 
  (Rs 100 Cr each), 11 users, and 23 marquee players.

- docker-compose.yml: 
  Runs MySQL 8.0 on port 3306 with database name ipl_auction_db.

8. TOP QUESTIONS THE MENTOR CAN ASK YOU & HOW TO ANSWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1: Why did you choose Pessimistic Locking over Optimistic Locking?
Ans: In an auction where 10 teams bid at the exact same second, Optimistic 
     Locking (using version numbers) would cause constant rollbacks and failed 
     clicks for users. Pessimistic Locking (SELECT ... FOR UPDATE) locks the row 
     briefly at the database level, evaluates the bid, commits, and releases 
     smoothly without throwing rollback exceptions to the user.

Q2: Why did you use BigDecimal instead of double or float for money?
Ans: In Java, double and float suffer from binary rounding errors (e.g., 
     100.0 - 0.2 = 99.80000000000001). In an auction with Rs 100 Crore budgets, 
     BigDecimal provides 100% exact mathematical accuracy.

Q3: What prevents a team from outbidding itself?
Ans: In BidService.java, we check the highest bid in the database. If 
     highestBid.getTeam().getId().equals(teamId), the system rejects the bid 
     with: 'You already hold the highest bid!'.

Q4: How do you prevent a team from bidding if they don't have enough purse?
Ans: Before saving any bid, we compare team.getBudget().compareTo(amount). 
     If the remaining purse is smaller than the bid amount, an exception is 
     thrown and the transaction rolls back.
